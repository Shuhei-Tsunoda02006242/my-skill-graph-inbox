Insider Brief
- A 10-member team from Chinese institutions won the 2026 ACM Gordon Bell Prize for developing the first classical solution at exascale to a simulation problem involving a key component of nitrogen fixation.
- The team modeled the electronic behavior of the iron-molybdenum cofactor, which helps an enzyme convert nitrogen into ammonia.
- The researchers tuned their code for two Chinese supercomputers and achieved a sustained 1.456 exaflops in double precision across 20,480 computing nodes on LineShine.
PRESS RELEASE — ACM, the Association for Computing Machinery, named a ten-member team drawn from Chinese institutions as the winner of the 2026 ACM Gordon Bell Prize for their project “Closing the FeMo-Cofactor ‘Quantum-Advantage’ Gap with Exascale Supercomputers.” The ACM Gordon Bell Prize tracks the progress of parallel computing and rewards innovation in applying high-performance computing to challenges in science, engineering, and large-scale data analytics.
In biochemistry, FeMo refers to the process by which nitrogen is converted into ammonia, a necessity for the growth of plants and other organisms. The effort to understand the intricate structure and function of this process is often called the FeMo Cofactor (FeMoco) CAS problem.
One way scientists have tried to tackle this challenge is by using high-performance computers to simulate the extremely complex interactions that make FeMo possible, including the interplay of atoms, molecules, and electronic states at the quantum level. It is believed that these simulations will yield insights that will benefit many other disciplines, including agriculture and medicine.
This year’s Gordon Bell Prize-winning team is recognized for developing the first “exascale” classical solution to the FeMoco CAS problem. Exascale computers, the world’s most powerful supercomputers, can perform up to a quintillion (a billion billion) calculations per second.
In the abstract of their paper, the team outlines the technical details of their achievement: “The resulting code has been tuned for two exascale supercomputers, LineShine and China’s New-generation Intelligent Super-computer (CNIS), reaching sustained performance on 20,480 LineShine nodes of 1.456 EFLOP/s in double precision (58.9% of the machine peak) and 5.626 EFLOP/s in mixed precision.”
The members of the ACM Gordon Bell Prize-Winning team are Honghui Shang, University of Science and Technology of China, Hefei; Zhiya Xiao, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing; Zhuozhao Xia, Hao Wu, and Huan Ma (all of University of Science and Technology of China, Hefei); Yongqing Chen and Bin Li (both of the National High Performance Computer Engineering Technology Research Center, Beijing); Zhenyu Li and Yutong Lu (both of Sun Yat-Sen University, Shenzhen); and Jinlong Yang, University of Science and Technology of China, Hefei.
The ACM Gordon Bell Prize was presented today during the International Conference for High-Performance Computing, Networking, Storage and Analysis (SC26) in Chicago, Illinois.
