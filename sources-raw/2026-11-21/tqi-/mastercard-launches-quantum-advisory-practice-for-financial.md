Insider Brief
- Mastercard has launched a Quantum Advisory Practice to help financial services organisations assess quantum-related cybersecurity risks and prepare for the transition to post-quantum cryptography.
- The advisory service will cover cryptographic inventories, PQC migration strategies, remediation roadmaps, executive education and potential business applications of quantum technology.
- Mastercard has also published a white paper on quantum readiness and is working with QIZ Security and Oxford Quantum Circuits on advisory services, research and a financial fraud protection pilot.
PRESS RELEASE — Quantum computing is not currently a mainstream technology, but cybersecurity experts and the financial services industry need to operate as if it’s here.
Quantum computing has transformative potential, from strengthening fraud detection to optimizing financial operations and loyalty, to unlocking new forms of innovation.
Yet, risk management is critical. Cybercriminals are preparing for a post-quantum future, in which powerful quantum technology could be used to access sensitive encrypted data and pose a threat to cryptography foundations that keep our financial systems secure. Today, there is already the serious potential of these bad actors hoarding this kind of data with the hopes of decrypting it in the future, a strategy known as “harvest now, decrypt later” (HNDL).
The industry needs to work to mitigate this threat, particularly as quantum technology continues to develop and we move towards a post-quantum era.
Building on years of work at the intersection of quantum innovation and cybersecurity, Mastercard is bringing together its expertise to help address both the opportunities and security implications of quantum advancements by launching a new Quantum Advisory Practice, part of its Advisors and Transformation consulting services, which connects advisory, innovation and deployment capabilities under a single operating model
This practice is designed to help financial services organizations assess their exposure to quantum-related risks and develop practical pathways toward post-quantum readiness, including post-quantum cryptography (PQC) planning and migration strategies, enterprise cryptographic inventory and remediation roadmaps, executive and board-level education, as well as identification of business opportunities emerging from quantum technologies.
Mastercard is already working with several partners on quantum readiness efforts, including QIZ Security, which will be part of the new advisory, and Oxford Quantum Circuits to collaborate on research and development – including a pilot on financial fraud protection.
To help organizations understand and prepare for this dynamic landscape, Mastercard today also published a new white paper, “Quantum computing and financial services,” which identifies near-term actions and longer-term plans to prepare for the post-quantum era.
“Quantum is already a present-day risk management and resilience imperative,” said Marc Carolus, EVP of Advisors & Transformation at Mastercard. “Mastercard has invested in technology capabilities, advisory services and partnerships to help the ecosystem navigate the quantum wave. With Mastercard’s new Quantum Advisory Practice, we’re helping organizations take deliberate steps today to understand their exposure, build cryptographic agility and embed long-term readiness into their enterprise strategy.”
Mastercard will continue working with our many partners to help the entire payments ecosystem and digital economy prepare for this future.
We’re grateful for the recognition earlier this week of being named No. 3 in financial services on the HorizonX’s 2026 Quantum Index, rising from No. 11 in 2025, and first place in the payments category. This ranking reflects our continued investment in quantum research, cybersecurity preparedness and commitment to helping customers navigate a quantum-enabled and post-quantum future.
