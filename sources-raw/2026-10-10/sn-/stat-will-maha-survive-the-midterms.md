WASHINGTON — Health secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.’s Make America Healthy Again movement rushed into American politics with a promise: Let an unlikely coalition of activists reach the masses, and they will deliver revolutionary gains for the Republican Party.
Yet as midterm elections draw near, the movement is nearly invisible on the campaign trail and struggling to find a financial or messaging foothold in key races. To some of its most vocal supporters, the very soul of MAHA is at stake come Nov. 3.
These problems are showing even as some of Kennedy’s ideas, including his critiques of the American food supply and corporate influence, continue to have widespread appeal. MAHA has yet to figure out how to turn those broadly popular themes into tangible policies or causes with political heft.
