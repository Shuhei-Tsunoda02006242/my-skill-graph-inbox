Insider Brief
- SAP and planqc expanded their collaboration and demonstrated a cloud interface linking SAP enterprise software to planqc’s neutral-atom quantum computing platform.
- In a live logistics demo, SAP sent a beverage truck-loading problem to a quantum simulator and received a loading plan back in its software.
- The companies plan to begin testing business applications on planqc’s neutral-atom quantum hardware in 2027.
PRESS RELEASE — SAP and planqc are expanding their collaboration in quantum computing. At the Quantum Day Munich by SAP, the two companies demonstrated the first live integration of a planqc neutral-atom quantum computer into an SAP environment. This marks an important step towards making quantum computing capabilities directly accessible to business applications.
The integration is enabled by a cloud interface connecting SAP applications with planqc’s quantum hardware. At the Quantum Day Munich by SAP, held at the SAP Campus in Garching, the companies also demonstrated how quantum computing can be incorporated into existing business processes through an initial logistics use case: optimizing the loading of delivery trucks.
Neutral-Atom Quantum Computing for Industrial Applications
Building on decades of world-class European research in quantum physics, planqc is turning scientific breakthroughs into powerful neutral-atom quantum computers. Its collaboration with SAP combines this technological expertise with SAP’s extensive experience in enterprise software, data, and business processes.
For SAP, expanding the collaboration is part of its broader quantum computing activities. The company expects quantum computers to reach performance levels relevant to SAP applications before the end of this decade. SAP is therefore actively exploring how quantum computing could complement its existing software portfolio and open up new approaches to solving complex business challenges.
For businesses, quantum computing holds particular promise for complex optimization, planning, and simulation tasks where classical computing methods may face limitations. Realizing this potential will depend not only on the performance of quantum hardware, but also on its integration into existing IT systems and business processes. This is where SAP and planqc bring together their complementary strengths.
“Europe has a strong research base and key parts of the technological supply chain needed to build quantum computers,” says Dr. Alexander Glätzle, Co-Founder and CEO of planqc. “The challenge now is to turn these strengths into competitive products and a sustainable industry. Together with SAP, we are bringing our neutral-atom quantum computers into the environments where companies manage their most complex business processes. Businesses need to build the necessary expertise and develop concrete use cases as the technology matures – not wait until powerful quantum computers become available.”
First Cloud Connection Between SAP and a Neutral-Atom Quantum Computer
At the heart of the integration is a cloud interface that directly connects the SAP software environment with planqc’s quantum hardware. It provides the technical foundation for SAP applications to send optimization tasks to quantum computing resources and incorporate the results back into existing business processes.
The architecture follows a hybrid approach, allowing SAP applications to combine classical computing resources, quantum simulators, and real quantum hardware. As quantum hardware becomes more powerful, increasingly complex tasks are expected to be executed on quantum processors. SAP is also using this foundation to develop its first Quantum Agents, with the long-term goal of creating applications that help SAP customers tackle particularly computationally demanding business challenges.
“Quantum computing opens up new possibilities for solving complex problems. We see neutral-atom quantum computers in particular as a promising approach to scalable quantum computing,” says Dr. Carsten Polenz, Global Head of Quantum at SAP. “Our priority now is to integrate these computing capabilities into business processes and make them accessible to customers. Working with planqc allows us to develop directly on real quantum hardware and accelerate the path towards practical business applications.”
First Logistics Use Case Demonstrated Live
At the Quantum Day Munich by SAP, the two companies demonstrated how quantum computing can be integrated into a real-world business process by optimizing the loading of a beverage delivery truck. During a live demonstration, the optimization task was transmitted directly from an SAP logistics process through the new interface to planqc’s quantum computing environment, where it was processed on a quantum simulator. The resulting loading plan was then transferred back into the SAP logistics process. This marked the first demonstration of an end-to-end workflow, with plans to use planqc’s real neutral-atom quantum hardware for such computations in the future.
The challenge is to allocate orders for kiosks, supermarkets, and food service businesses to a vehicle’s loading compartments while making the best possible use of the available capacity. Several constraints must be taken into account, including compartment capacity, preferred unloading side, and refrigeration requirements. Products that belong together should be placed in the same compartment. With every additional order, the number of possible loading configurations grows exponentially. These so-called bin-packing problems are among the complex optimization challenges for which quantum computers could offer new approaches in the future.
The economic potential is significant: more efficient loading could reduce transportation costs, improve vehicle utilization, and simplify logistics operations. This use case illustrates the economic value that quantum computing could create within existing business processes in the future. Further application testing will explore the extent to which quantum computing methods can offer advantages over classical approaches.
Next Steps: Business Applications on planqc Hardware from 2027
The integration marks an important technical milestone for SAP and planqc. Starting in 2027, the companies plan to test initial business scenarios on real neutral-atom quantum hardware from planqc. In parallel, SAP is expanding its presence in the regional quantum ecosystem with a dedicated quantum team in Munich. The region is one of the leading centers for neutral-atom quantum computing, bringing together world-class research, specialized suppliers, and technology companies such as planqc. Both companies aim to identify and develop concrete use cases at an early stage, helping pave the way for the industrial adoption of quantum computing.
