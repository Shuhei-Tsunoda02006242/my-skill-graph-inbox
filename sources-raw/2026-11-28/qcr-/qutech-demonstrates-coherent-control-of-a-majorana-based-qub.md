Researchers at QuTech and the Kavli Institute of Nanoscience at Delft University of Technology, working with Eindhoven University of Technology, have demonstrated coherent time-domain control of a parity qubit encoded in Majorana quasiparticles, publishing their experimental results in Nature. Fabricated in an indium antimonide (InSb) nanowire proximitized by a superconducting aluminum loop, the device integrates two minimal two-site Kitaev chains hosting four Majorana modes (γ1 through γ4), a central coupler quantum dot to mediate inter-chain tunneling, and an auxiliary parity-switching dot that maps odd-parity states for single-shot dispersive readout via radio-frequency quantum capacitance.

|---|---|
| Parameter / Metric | Experimental Value & Configuration | 
| Device Architecture | Two 2-site Kitaev chains (4 Majorana modes γ1–γ4) in an 85 nm InSb nanowire with a 15 nm Al superconducting loop (~0.4 μm2) | 
| Operating Conditions | ~20 mK base temperature; 150 mT parallel magnetic field (B\|\|); 500 μs single-shot RF quantum-capacitance readout | 
| Encoded Basis States | Global even parity manifold (\|ee⟩, \|oo⟩) and global odd parity manifold (\|eo⟩, \|oe⟩) | 
| Sweet-Spot Rabi Frequencies | 113.4 ± 0.5 MHz (even manifold) and 117.7 ± 0.4 MHz (odd manifold), matching within ~5% across coupler amplitudes | 
| Coherence & Phase Control | Rabi decay time T2Rabi = 26 ± 2 ns at 106.7 MHz; periodic coupling modulation suppressed at superconducting phase difference φ = π | 
Operating at 20 mK, the team tuned both Kitaev chains to their Majorana sweet spots where elastic co-tunneling and crossed-Andreev reflection balance, then pulsed the central coupler dot to drive coherent Rabi oscillations between computational parity states. Measurements across both the global even (|ee⟩, |oo⟩) and odd (|eo⟩, |oe⟩) manifolds yielded matching oscillation frequencies of 113.4 MHz and 117.7 MHz, confirming minimal wavefunction overlap between outer Majorana modes, while sweeping an out-of-plane magnetic field verified periodic superconducting phase control that suppresses inter-chain coupling at a phase difference of π.
Although minimal two-site chains host “poor man’s Majoranas” with only partial protection against local charge noise, resulting in coherence times on the order of tens of nanoseconds, detuning individual quantum dots confirmed that the encoded qubit state is delocalized across all four chain sites. QuTech researchers are now extending the platform to longer multi-dot Kitaev chains, starting with three-dot-per-chain arrays, to test whether greater spatial separation exponentially suppresses decoherence and enables non-Abelian braiding operations.
