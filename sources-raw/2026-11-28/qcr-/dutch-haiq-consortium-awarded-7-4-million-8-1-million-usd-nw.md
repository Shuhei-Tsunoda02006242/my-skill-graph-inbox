A 28-partner Dutch consortium led by Leiden University researchers Dr. Evert van Nieuwenburg and Professor Julia Cramer has been awarded €7.4 million ($8.1 Million USD) (plus €50,000 ($54.7K USD) in in-kind co-funding from Quantum Delta NL) by the Dutch Research Council (NWO) under its NWA-ORC program for the Human and Artificial Intelligence Advancing Quantum Technology (HAIQ) project. Bringing together QuTech, TU Delft, TU Eindhoven, SURF, Quantum Delta NL, game studios, and Dutch quantum hardware and control companies including Qblox, Orange Quantum Systems, Groove Quantum, and QuLearnLabs, the initiative pairs human-in-the-loop gamification with machine learning to tackle unstructured optimization bottlenecks in quantum computing.
Building on earlier human-AI quantum error-correction prototypes such as Leiden’s ERRATIQ game, QuTech researchers Sebastian Feld, Anasua Chatterjee, Eliška Greplová, and Vivek Sinha will focus on applying human-guided reinforcement learning to quantum circuit compilation, device calibration, and real-time qubit stabilization while upgrading QuTech’s cloud-based Quantum Inspire platform so participants can design circuits and run experiments directly on quantum hardware.
