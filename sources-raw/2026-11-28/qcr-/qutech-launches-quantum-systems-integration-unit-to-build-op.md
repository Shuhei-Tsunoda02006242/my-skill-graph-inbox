QuTech, the joint quantum research institute of Delft University of Technology (TU Delft) and TNO, has launched a dedicated Quantum Systems Integration Unit to design, assemble, and commission full-stack, open-architecture superconducting quantum computers built on a European supply chain. Seed-funded by QuTech Business Development alongside public-private partnership support from Holland High Tech, Quantum Delta NL’s CAT-1 program, the Dutch Research Council (NWO), and the EU Quantum Flagship’s OpenSuperQPlus initiative, the unit is led by Professor Leo DiCarlo as Scientific Lead, Vivek Sinha as Strategy and Partnerships Lead, and Rebecca Mayrhofer as Head of Business Development.

Rather than treating quantum chip fabrication in isolation, the unit coordinates the complete hardware and software stack across cryogenics, packaging, microwave wiring, control electronics, automated calibration, compilers, monitoring, and cloud access. Building on five generations of processors deployed on QuTech’s open-access Quantum Inspire platform, including the 17-qubit Tuna-17 system released in July 2026 and the HectoQubit/2 program, the team is collaborating with European specialists including TNO, QuantWare, Qblox, Delft Circuits, Orange Quantum Systems, Peak Quantum, and Riverlane to establish standardized, modular interfaces.
To translate academic quantum error-correction breakthroughs into repeatable commercial systems, the unit directly incorporates recent DiCarlo Lab research on measurement-integrated leakage reduction units, tunable-coupler frequency positioning against spectator-qubit crosstalk, and ionizing-radiation error-burst mitigation. Over its first year, the team will formalize system-integration blueprints and intellectual property across turnkey systems, integration-as-a-service, and high-performance computing (HPC) deployments ahead of a planned commercial spinout venture in 2028.
