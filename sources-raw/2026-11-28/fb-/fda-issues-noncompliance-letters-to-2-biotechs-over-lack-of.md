The FDA has issued two biotechs with noncompliance letters for failure to report clinical trial results.
The agency can issue these letters if the trial investigator or sponsor has failed to register an applicable clinical trial or submit required clinical trial information to the ClinicalTrials.gov database, or if they knowingly submitted false or misleading information. According to the agency, if the responsible party does not take action within 30 days after receiving the notice, the FDA can pursue regulatory or enforcement actions.
Federal law requires that trial results information be submitted to ClinicalTrials.gov regardless of whether a trial was completed or terminated early. The FDA issued its first noncompliance letter back in 2021 and has now issued a total of eleven letters to biopharmas, foundations and individual investigators.
The most recent letters—which were first reported by Stat—were sent to Duchesnay and Addex Pharma in October. Fierce has reached out to both companies for more details on whether they plan to respond.
Duchesnay, which is focused on developing medications that are safe for use during pregnancy, was issued a noncompliance letter on Oct. 19 for failure to report data to ClinicalTrials.gov for its phase 4 study to treat nausea and vomiting in pregnant teens. According to the trials database, the study was terminated in 2017, but the company did not list a reason.
In Duchesnay’s letter, the FDA noted that on March 17 it had requested records of the clinical trial to be reported, but the company had still not submitted the trial data by the time that the agency sent its letter in October.
The second batch of letters went out to Addex Pharma for a pair of trials the Swiss biotech is running for patients with Parkinson’s disease. Addex received its first notice from the FDA back in July 2025. But the agency said in its letters last month that “despite multiple emails exchanged between your company and the FDA … results information has not been submitted.”
The last time that the FDA issued a noncompliance letter specifically to a biotech was back in March 2025, when Mezzion Pharma failed to report data results for its phase 3 study in adults who underwent a specific type of open-heart surgery. In January 2026, the agency issued a letter to Mezzion stating that the “company has addressed the issues raised in the FDA's letter.”
