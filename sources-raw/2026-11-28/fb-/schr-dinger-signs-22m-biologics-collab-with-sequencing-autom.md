Computational discovery biotech Schrödinger has finalized a biologics-focused collaboration with sequencing automation company Enpicom in an all-cash deal worth 22 million euros ($24 million).
Netherlands-based Enpicom uses bioinformatics and AI to push biologics discovery along at a faster pace.
Meanwhile, Schrödinger has already brought two drug candidates into early-stage clinical trials in the form of the MALT1 inhibitor SGR-1505 for non-Hodgkin’s B-cell lymphomas and the Wee1/Myt1 inhibitor SGR-3515 for ovarian and uterine cancer. Both programs are in phase 1, heading a lineup of preclinical and discovery-stage small molecule candidates for neurodegenerative, cardiovascular, inflammation, immunology and oncology indications.
Schrödinger is leveraging the deal with Enpicom to forge into the world of biologics, having focused on small molecules to this point, Schrödinger CEO Ramy Farid, Ph.D., said in a statement.
“Uniting Enpicom’s bioinformatics software with our computational platform and enterprise solutions will enable us to support biologics discovery campaigns from initial screening through atomic-scale optimization, while providing the underlying architecture to further expand into new modalities,” Farid added.
Enpicom CEO Nicola Bonzanni said that the company’s bioinformatics platform “turns weeks of complex sequence analysis into hours of decisive insight, matching software and automation directly to the needs of biologics discovery teams.”
Schrödinger is essentially a software company that uses a simulation technique to understand small molecule behavior at the atomic level, having sent several of its backed companies into the hands of pharmaceutical giant Eli Lilly, including a 2013 deal with Nimbus Therapeutics, followed by the acquisitions of Morphic in 2024 and Ajax Therapeutics earlier this year.
