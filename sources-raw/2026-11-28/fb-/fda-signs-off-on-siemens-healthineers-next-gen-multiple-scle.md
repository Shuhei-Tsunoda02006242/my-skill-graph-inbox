The FDA has given de novo marketing authorization for Siemens Healthineers’ Atellica IM Neurofilament Light Chain (NfL) assay.
The novel blood test helps evaluate ongoing disease activity in patients aged 18 to 55 years with relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (RRMS). This expands access to a disease management tool already used by clinicians outside the U.S. under CE mark, the company said in a release.
After sharing a weaker-than-expected financial outlook for FY2027, Siemens Healthineers recently announced that it is carving out its underperforming diagnostics business into a standalone company called Olyrian Diagnostics. The company will also change its name to “Healthineers.”
“This first-of-its-kind NfL blood test for MS offers a new, non-invasive way to gain valuable patient health data, supporting more informed clinical decision making,” said Sharon Bracken, head of Siemens Healthineers Diagnostics, soon to be Olyrian Diagnostics, in a statement.
“We’re translating innovation in brain health into clinical practice, expanding access to automated neurological biomarker testing, and ultimately empowering better patient care.”
According to the National Multiple Sclerosis Society research, MS affects approximately 1 million people in the United States, with RRMS2 cases accounting for 85% of initial MS diagnoses.
Monitoring the disease can be challenging because critical changes are not always apparent through clinical observation alone. When neuroaxonal injury occurs, NfL is released into cerebrospinal fluid and subsequently into the blood. The assay detects and measures the neurofilament light chain in blood.
The Atellica IM NfL assay is a fully automated chemiluminescent sandwich immunoassay performed on the Atellica IM Analyzer. Neurological biomarker testing performed in an automated laboratory environment enables blood testing to occur alongside clinical assessment.
The assay, developed in collaboration with Novartis, measures NfL in human serum and EDTA plasma and is intended for use in conjunction with clinical, imaging, and lab findings.
