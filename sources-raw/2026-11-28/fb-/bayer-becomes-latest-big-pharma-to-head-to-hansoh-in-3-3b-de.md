Bayer has become the latest drug giant to turn to Hansoh Pharma for its antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) expertise.
The German pharma is paying $150 million upfront for the ex-China rights to HS-20122, an EGFR/c-Met-targeted bispecific ADC aimed at solid tumors like non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Hansoh has already taken HS-20122 through a phase 1 study, for which it presented “encouraging efficacy and safety results," the company said in a Nov. 25 release.
Specifically, the Chinese company’s presentation at the European Society for Medical Oncology 2026 Congress last month linked HS-20122 to an overall response rate (ORR) of 61.3%, and a six-month progression-free survival rate of 75.3%. At the time, Hansoh suggested these data positioned the therapy as a “novel option” for patients with EGFR mutant NSCLC.
The data apparently were also enough to convince Bayer. As well as the upfront fee, Wednesday’s licensing deal comes with potential milestone payments that could reach $3.1 billion, in addition to tiered royalties on future sales.
When it comes to oncology, Bayer markets the prostate cancer drug Nubeqa and the NSCLC therapy Hyrnuo. The company’s clinical-stage pipeline includes the WRN covalent inhibitor VVD-133214 and the radiopharmaceutical therapy BAY 3547926.
“We are excited to further advance HS-20122, a next-generation, bispecific ADC, together with Hansoh Pharma,” Juergen Eckhardt, M.D., head of business development and licensing at Bayer’s pharmaceuticals division, said in a statement.
“Despite treatment advances, people living with lung cancer continue to need new options that offer greater efficacy and improved tolerability,” Eckhardt added. “This collaboration supports Bayer’s precision oncology strategy by advancing ADCs that more selectively target tumor cells to deliver cancer-killing payloads.”
Amid Big Pharma’s race to scoop up as many innovative medicines from China as possible, Hansoh’s ADCs are some of the hottest tickets going. Last month, Roche returned to the Jiangsu-based company for a mammoth $15 billion biobucks pact spanning a “broad portfolio of ADC programs.”
Meanwhile, GSK has already tasted clinical success with a pair of Hansoh ADCs aimed at B7-H3 and B7-H4, respectively. The B7-H3-directed ADC, dubbed ris-rez, recently achieved a median overall survival of 18.5 months in a phase 3 trial, lining up the therapy as a potential threat to Amgen and Merck & Co. in the lung cancer field. Last month, GSK unveiled the first global data for ris-rez, which demonstrated a 61% overall response rate in a phase 1b trial.
Eliza Sun, executive director of Hansoh’s board, said in Wednesday’s release that HS-20122 had “demonstrated promising clinical activity across multiple tumor types.”
“Building on Hansoh Pharma’s novel ADC platform and extensive portfolio in lung cancer and other solid tumors, we look forward to working closely together to advance this innovative therapy and deliver meaningful benefits to patients worldwide,” Sun added.
