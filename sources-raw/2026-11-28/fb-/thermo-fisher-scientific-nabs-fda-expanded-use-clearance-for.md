Thermo Fisher Scientific announced FDA clearance for expanded intended use of its EXENT Analyser with the EXENT GAM Assay.
The change will allow clinical labs to use mass spectrometry-based testing to aid in monitoring of disease status and treatment response in patients with multiple myeloma and Waldenström's macroglobulinemia.
Building on the EXENT system’s previous clearance to aid in diagnosis, the expanded use is designed to help clinicians detect and track monoclonal proteins over time, including at low concentrations, and distinguish disease-related proteins from certain known therapeutic antibodies.
The expanded clearance addresses a critical challenge in managing complex blood cancers by giving clinicians greater insight into how a patient's disease is changing and responding to treatment.
By bringing sensitive, specific mass spectrometry-based testing directly into clinical laboratories, the EXENT system enables healthcare providers to monitor patients over time using a single platform, from initial diagnosis through ongoing disease management, Thermo Fisher said in a release.
“For patients living with multiple myeloma or Waldenström's macroglobulinemia, understanding how their disease is responding to treatment is critical,” said Stephen Harding, vice president and general manager, protein diagnostics, Thermo Fisher Scientific, in a statement.
“This expanded clearance brings the precision of mass spectrometry beyond diagnosis and into ongoing monitoring, giving clinicians greater insight into disease status and treatment response.”
Multiple myeloma and Waldenström’s macroglobulinemia are both monoclonal gammopathies that require frequent, sensitive monitoring of M-protein levels to assess disease status, treatment response, and detect potential relapse.
Patients are typically monitored for M-protein levels throughout their lifetime following diagnosis. According to the National Cancer Institute, an estimated 36,000 new cases of multiple myeloma occurred in the United States in 2026.
