Germany and Japan have reaffirmed their commitment to advancing fusion energy through cooperation following German Chancellor Friedrich Merz’s visit to Tokyo on October 28, 2026. During a meeting with Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, the two leaders issued a joint declaration on advanced technology cooperation and identified fusion energy as a priority area for collaboration.
The Japan-Germany Joint Leaders’ Statement emphasized strengthening cooperation in emerging technologies, including fusion energy, as central to bilateral relations. An accompanying Joint Leaders’ Declaration on Advanced Technology Cooperation welcomed preparations for an agreement between Japan’s Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT) and Germany’s Federal Ministry of Research, Technology and Space (BMFTR) to deepen fusion cooperation, including stellarator and laser-based approaches.
The joint declaration specifically recognized six industry and research agreements:
- Kyoto Fusioneering and Proxima Fusion signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU) to expand cooperation on plasma heating systems and fusion fuel cycle technologies.
- Kyoto Fusioneering and Focused Energy signed an MoU to explore collaboration on fusion technologies, including fuel cycle systems, materials, and diagnostics.
- Proxima Fusion and Fujikura signed an MoU to explore developing high-temperature superconducting tape manufacturing capacity in Germany.
- Helical Fusion and Gauss Fusion signed a joint statement of intent to explore cooperation on stellarator fusion technologies.
- Focused Energy and Osaka University’s Institute of Laser Engineering signed an MoU supporting cooperation in laser fusion research and development.
- Mitsui & Co., Kyoto Fusioneering, and Siemens Energy signed an MoU, further expanding industrial cooperation between Japan and Germany.
Including fusion energy in the countries’ bilateral technology agenda reflects growing government recognition of the industry’s potential and the importance of public-private cooperation in supporting its development.
Together, these agreements reflect growing collaboration among fusion developers, suppliers, and research institutions in addressing the scientific and engineering challenges of commercial fusion.
