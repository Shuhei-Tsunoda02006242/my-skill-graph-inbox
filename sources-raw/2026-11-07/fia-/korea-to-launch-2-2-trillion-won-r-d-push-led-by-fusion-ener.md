South Korea will begin rolling out 2.2 trillion won ($1.5 billion) worth of national research and development projects next year, including a 1.5 trillion won program to develop core nuclear fusion technologies.
The Ministry of Science and ICT said on the 5th that it held the ninth Oversight Committee on National R&D Program Evaluation of 2026 in written form over three days from the 3rd to the 5th, chaired by Park In-kyu, head of the Office of Science, Technology and Innovation. The committee finalized the results of the fourth preliminary feasibility study of 2025 and the first of 2026.
All three projects reviewed had been filed before the preliminary feasibility review system for R&D was abolished, and the government decided to proceed with all of them.
The largest of the approved projects is the science ministry's program to develop core technologies and build demonstration infrastructure to accelerate fusion energy. Total project costs reach 1.4813 trillion won, of which 1.2877 trillion won will come from the state budget. The program runs for 10 years, from 2027 through 2036.
Through the project, the government plans to advance core fusion energy technologies — including core plasma, heating and current drive, superconducting magnets, breeding blankets, fusion materials and the fuel cycle — to a level verified under extreme conditions similar to those inside a fusion reactor. It will also build a demonstration center for core fusion energy technologies to verify research results and lay the technical groundwork for commercialization.
The push comes as the expansion of artificial intelligence and data centers makes securing a competitive edge in future energy technologies more important. "We have supported the development of various original technologies in the fusion field, but accelerating the realization of fusion energy requires advancing core technologies and building demonstration infrastructure for performance evaluation," the science ministry said.
The government also decided to proceed with two other projects that were the last to be filed before the R&D preliminary feasibility system was scrapped: a program to develop molten salt reactor (MSR) technology for maritime use in pursuit of carbon neutrality, and a project to build a national research platform for advanced displays.
The MSR program is being pursued jointly by the science ministry and the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries. The government plans to use it to lay the groundwork for commercializing MSR-based nuclear-powered vessels, which are under consideration as one option for decarbonization. Total project costs come to 317.3 billion won, including 225.2 billion won from the state budget. The government will develop core technologies such as the reactor core and systems, nuclear fuel and materials for maritime MSRs, along with reactor-vessel interface and safety assessment technologies for MSR-powered ships. The program runs four years, from next year through 2030.
The advanced display research platform, pursued by the Ministry of Trade and Industry at a total cost of 368.9 billion won including 278.9 billion won in state funding, aims to concentrate R&D capabilities in the display sector. To strengthen competitiveness in the global market, the government will focus research on three technology themes: "over OLED," which seeks to overcome the performance limits of existing organic light-emitting diodes; "cross OLED," which combines various functions and technologies; and "post OLED," which targets next-generation technologies beyond OLED. The project will run seven years, from next year through 2033, and will include demonstration infrastructure covering the full cycle from design to panel-level testing.
"The fusion and MSR projects are core technology areas included in the government's seven SEED projects, and the display project is a field where Korea leads the global market," Park said. "I have asked the ministries in charge to move quickly so that the country can secure next-generation growth engines early." Park added, "We will continue to strengthen strategic investment in national R&D, including the seven SEED projects, and translate it into the creation of future industries."
