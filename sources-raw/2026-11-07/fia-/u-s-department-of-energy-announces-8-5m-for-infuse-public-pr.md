The U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) Office of Science announced $8.5 million in fiscal year 2026 funding for 28 projects through the Innovation Network for Fusion Energy (INFUSE) program. The selected projects will connect private fusion companies with DOE national laboratories and U.S. universities to address critical scientific and technological challenges and accelerate the path toward commercialization. The FY 2027 INFUSE Request for Assistance (RFA) is also now open, with applications due by December 13, 2026.
The INFUSE program supports public-private partnerships that bring together the technical expertise and research capabilities of national laboratories and universities with the commercialization efforts of private fusion companies. The latest selections include 17 national laboratory-led projects and 11 university-led projects involving 22 commercial enterprises.
The projects will advance research across several areas critical to commercial fusion development, including modeling and simulation, materials science, plasma diagnostics, and superconducting magnets. These partnerships help address shared technical challenges across the fusion industry. By expanding access to specialized research facilities, expertise, and infrastructure, INFUSE helps private companies advance technologies needed to develop commercially viable fusion energy systems.

FIA Affiliate Members: EPRI, SuperPower Inc., and Westinghouse Electric Company
FIA Education & Research Partners: Oak Ridge National Laboratory, Colorado State University, Columbia University, Idaho National Laboratory, Los Alamos National Laboratory, Massachusetts Institute of Technology, and the University of Wisconsin–Madison.
