South Korea’s Yonsei University and IBM have completed the on-premises installation and online activation of the latest IBM Quantum Nighthawk r2 processor within the IBM Quantum System One at the Songdo International Campus Quantum Computing Center. Replacing the 127-qubit IBM Eagle processor originally commissioned in 2024, the installation marks the world’s first deployment of IBM’s Nighthawk architecture at an on-premises client facility outside the United States.
| Yonsei IBM Quantum System One: Hardware Architecture Evolution |  |  | 
|---|---|---|
| Platform Dimension | Previous Baseline (IBM Eagle) | Upgraded Architecture (IBM Nighthawk r2) | 
| Qubit Layout & Topology | 127 physical qubits (Heavy-hexagonal lattice) | 120 programmable qubits within a 458-physical-qubit square lattice (including tunable couplers and reset elements). | 
| Circuit Execution Throughput | ~4,000 circuits per second | > 100,000 circuits per second (~25× throughput acceleration enabled by fast active qubit reset). | 
| Depth & Gate Complexity | Depth-limited by two-qubit error propagation | Accurate observable estimation demonstrated beyond 7,500 gates (satisfying IBM’s 2026 technical roadmap target). | 
| Primary Research Focus | Initial benchmarking and condensed matter physics | Yonsei Bio-Quantum Initiative (mitochondrial electron transport chain mutations and Leigh Syndrome modeling). | 
The upgrade transitions the Songdo facility from the heavy-hexagonal layout of Eagle to IBM’s dense square-lattice geometry. By incorporating 218 tunable couplers alongside dedicated fast-reset auxiliary circuits, Nighthawk r2 suppresses idle crosstalk and dramatically curtails the shot-repetition cycle time. The resulting execution speed of 100,000 circuits per second provides local researchers with the sample collection rates needed for high-depth variational algorithms and scalable error-mitigated workflows without incurring prohibitive cloud queue overheads.
Since commissioning the original System One, the Yonsei research community has generated over a dozen peer-reviewed studies on IBM hardware, including a 104-qubit analysis of error-mitigated quench dynamics in Heisenberg spin chains published in Physica Scripta, alongside a 44-qubit hybrid quantum-classical simulation of the pyridine–Li+ complex detailed on arXiv utilizing Qunova’s Hi-VQE Qiskit Function. Under the expanded partnership, the Nighthawk r2 installation will primarily support the Yonsei Bio-Quantum Initiative, applying hybrid quantum-classical workflows to complex cellular kinetics, neurodegenerative disorders, and industrial materials discovery.
