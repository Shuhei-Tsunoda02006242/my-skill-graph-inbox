In its newly published 2026 Annual Report, the National Quantum Computing Centre (NQCC) has outlined a programmatic pivot across the UK’s national quantum ecosystem, transitioning public strategy from exploratory technology development toward active procurement, system benchmarking, and industrial adoption. Operating from its national laboratory facilities at the Harwell Science and Innovation Campus, the NQCC coupled its annual review with two operational milestones: launching the inaugural cohort for its SparQ Access Industry Channel and awarding grants to 13 enterprise-led projects under its 2026 Proof of Concept (PoC and PoC+) Call.

A central component of the NQCC’s operational milestone is the transition from the initial deployment phase of the Quantum Computing Testbed Initiative (QCTI) to performance verification and the newly structured ProQure framework. Co-delivered with Innovate UK, the testbed facilities host seven commercial quantum computers across five physical modalities at Harwell: trapped ions (Oxford Ionics, now IonQ), superconducting circuits (Rigetti UK), neutral atoms (QuEra UK and Infleqtion UK), silicon spin qubits (Quantum Motion), and photonics (ORCA Computing and Aegiq). In partnership with the University of Edinburgh’s Quantum Software Lab (QSL), the NQCC is benchmarking these platforms to establish independent verification criteria. Under ProQure’s sovereign procurement roadmap, these metrics will inform government procurement under a planned £1 billion Advanced Market Commitment intended to deploy large-scale machines capable of executing one billion quantum operations by 2031.
To broaden industrial access ahead of fault-tolerant systems, the NQCC launched the SparQ Access Industry Channel, which provides selected commercial organizations with centrally brokered access to commercial quantum processors. The inaugural nine-member cohort includes multinational pharmaceutical developer AstraZeneca, commodities trading firm Powertica Commodities, fault-tolerant software developer Riverlane, Applied Quantum Computing, Kū Quantum AI, Nexorion Spacetech, Qatalyst-Quantum, Quantum Base Alpha, and Softomate Solutions. Across its broader academic and public tiers, the SparQ platform currently brokers access to 22 distinct quantum processing units across 76 institutions, building upon multi-partner industrial collaborations in energy optimization.
In parallel, the NQCC and the Science and Technology Facilities Council (STFC) confirmed 13 collaborative R&D awards through the 2026 SparQ Proof of Concept call, expanding upon the initial cohort of 12 SparQ Proof-of-Concept projects with a 44% increase in application volume. The call introduced a dual-track structure: a standard three-month PoC track for rapid feasibility assessments, and a six-month PoC+ track designed to test algorithmic scaling using real-world enterprise datasets. Funded initiatives include projects led by Q-CTRL (single-atom catalysis), PsiQuantum (infrastructure corrosion modeling), Qoro Quantum (energy grid resilience), Curenetics (neoantigen vaccine design), and IonQ (standby flight crew optimization).
