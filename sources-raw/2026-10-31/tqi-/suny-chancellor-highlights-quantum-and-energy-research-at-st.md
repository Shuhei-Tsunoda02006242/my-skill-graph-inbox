Insider Brief
- SUNY Chancellor John B. King Jr. visited Stony Brook University to review research across quantum computing, energy, healthcare, artificial intelligence and environmental science.
- During the visit, King toured the Advanced Energy Research and Technology Center, attended a research expo and met with faculty working on projects including distributed quantum processing, nuclear energy, battery technology and clean water.
- The visit highlighted SUNY’s efforts to expand research across its university system, including Stony Brook’s Quantum Research and Innovation Hub and collaborations with Brookhaven National Laboratory.
PRESS RELEASE — State University of New York Chancellor John B. King Jr. today highlighted the State University of New York at Stony Brook’s groundbreaking research with a tour of research facilities and campus laboratories. During his visit, Chancellor King was joined by SUNY Trustee Cary Staller and toured the Advanced Energy Research and Technology Center, attended a research expo featuring faculty and student projects, and engaged with researchers on a wide range of topics, from healthy aging and mental health to nuclear energy and clean water.
“SUNY students and faculty are at the vanguard of vital research efforts for the public good,” said SUNY Chancellor King. “At the State University of New York at Stony Brook, researchers are addressing complex global challenges, opening the door to significant opportunities for our students, and economic growth for our communities.”
SUNY Trustee Cary Staller said, “The State University of New York at Stony Brook is the epitome of research and innovation that positions New York State as a global leader. Through state-of-the-art research facilities and cutting-edge projects, we are expanding knowledge, creating better health outcomes, strengthening communities, and cultivating workforce opportunities.”
During his visit, Chancellor King met with faculty researchers to discuss projects addressing scientific and societal challenges, including:
- Eden Figueroa, PhD, is the Presidential Professor of Physics, Director of the Stony Brook University Quantum Institute, and a joint appointee at Brookhaven National Laboratory. He is the Director of the Center for Distributed Quantum Processing, and he and his former students patented Qu-MEM, which was recently named one of TIME Magazine’s Best Inventions.
- Pamela Abshire, PhD, Chair of the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, is internationally known for her work in low power mixed-signal integrated circuits (IC), adaptive ICs and IC sensors, and CMOS biosensors. Her research focuses on better understanding and exploiting the tradeoffs between performance and resources in natural and engineered systems.
- Christine DeLorenzo, PhD, interim co-director of the Center for Healthy Aging at Stony Brook and professor in the Department of Psychiatry and Behavioral Health and Department of Biomedical Engineering in the Renaissance School of Medicine, whose research advances precision medicine by applying advanced neuroimaging to uncover the biological bases of mood disorders and neurological diseases.
- Anissa Abi-Dargham, MD, SUNY Distinguished Professor of Psychiatry and Radiology, the Lourie Endowed Chair in Psychiatry, associate dean and associate vice president for clinical and translational science and chair of the Department of Psychiatry and Behavioral Health in the Renaissance School of Medicine, whose research uses positron emission tomography (PET) imaging to study the neurobiology of schizophrenia and other mental illnesses.
- Erich R. Mackow, PhD, professor in the Department of Microbiology and Immunology in the Renaissance School of Medicine, whose laboratory investigates viral cell targeting and the regulation of vascular, pulmonary and central nervous system functions to identify potential therapeutics and vaccines for viral pulmonary and neurodegenerative diseases.
- Luis A. Marcos, MD, associate professor of medicine and director of the Infectious Diseases Fellowship Program at Stony Brook Medicine, whose research focuses on tick-borne diseases, including Lyme disease and babesiosis, in Suffolk County.
- SUNY alumnus Lance Snead, PhD, research professor in the Department of Materials Science and Chemical Engineering in the College of Engineering and Applied Sciences, whose research focuses on radiation-resistant materials for nuclear fusion and fission energy systems.
- David Sprouster, PhD, assistant professor in the Department of Materials Science and Chemical Engineering in the College of Engineering and Applied Sciences, whose research focuses on developing next-generation energy materials capable of withstanding the extreme environments associated with advanced nuclear fission and fusion reactors.
- SUNY alumnus Raymond C. Bergan, MD, the Kavita and Lalit Bahl Endowed Cancer Center Director at Stony Brook Cancer Center, vice dean for Cancer Medicine and vice president for Cancer Services, whose research focuses on cancer metastasis. His team discovered the first precision-acting drug to inhibit cancer cell movement and metastasis and identified its mechanism of action.
- Lokesh Padhye, PhD, associate professor and associate director of Emerging Contaminant Research at the New York State Center for Clean Water Technology, whose research focuses on improving water quality by identifying and removing emerging contaminants, including PFAS, microplastics and pharmaceuticals.
- Esther Takeuchi, PhD, chair of the Interdisciplinary Science Department at Brookhaven National Laboratory and the William and Jane Knapp Chair in Energy and the Environment and Distinguished Professor in Stony Brook’s College of Engineering and Applied Sciences, whose pioneering battery technology has helped power life-saving implantable cardiac defibrillators and whose current research focuses on advancing battery technology for energy storage.
- SUNY alumna Amy Marschilok, PhD, manager of the Energy Storage Division at Brookhaven National Laboratory and professor of chemistry in Stony Brook’s College of Arts and Sciences, whose research focuses on developing safer, more efficient and longer-lasting batteries for advanced energy storage applications.
- Donghee Yvette Wohn, inaugural chair of the Department of Technology, AI and Society in the College of Engineering and Applied Sciences, whose research explores human-computer interaction, artificial intelligence and online social behavior, with a focus on how emerging technologies shape digital communities, identity and social interactions.
