A new year brings a fresh Fierce Biotech Fundraising Tracker designed to record the significant amount of venture capital that flows into biopharma.
We’re keeping the same guidelines used for last year’s tracker and will only include financing rounds of $50 million and above.
October
October 16—Estuary Biotherapeutics
Series: A
Amount: $93M
Investors: RA Capital Management, Deep Track Capital, Biogen New Ventures, UPMC Enterprises, Pivotal bioVenture Partners, the National Kidney Foundation Innovation Fund, a family office connected to an individual living with autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD), 4BIO Capital and GV.
Estuary's series A, combined with $10 million in seed funding, meant the biotech emerged from stealth with $103 million on hand to power a pair of gene therapies into clinical trials for an inherited kidney disorder. Story
October 15—OMass Therapeutics
Series: C
Amount: $310M
Investors: GV, Bain Capital Life Sciences, Forbion, Novo Holdings, Janus Henderson Investors, SV Health Investors, Jefferson Life Sciences, Syncona, Sanofi Ventures, the U.K. government’s British Business Bank and the University of Oxford
British biotech OMass will channel its new series C funds toward taking its lead candidate—a melanocortin-2 receptor antagonist dubbed OMS1620—through an ongoing phase 1 study and into phase 2 development in congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) and ACTH-dependent Cushing’s syndrome. The Oxford, U.K.-based startup has hopes to become “Britain's Big Biotech," as the U.K. has not seen a success story in the space on the scale of those written by Vertex or Genentech. The biotech's fundraising history includes a $100 million series B from 2022 that was topped off with another 10 million pounds sterling in 2023. Story
October 13—Soufflé Therapeutics
Series: B
Amount: $135M
Investors: Juvia Group, TCG Crossover Management, BB Biotech, Fidelity Management & Research Company, Balyasny Asset Management, a Leading Sovereign Wealth Fund, Amgen Ventures, ARCH Venture Partners, Bessemer Venture Partners, Breyer Capital, The Invus Group, Janus Henderson, Leaps by Bayer, Polaris Partners, Safar Partners, TAS Partners, Temasek and Vida Ventures
Soufflé has risen even further, following a $200 million series A when the Boston biotech emerged from stealth in October 2025. The oligonucleotide therapy startup is a Langer family affair, with Moderna maverick Robert Langer co-founding the company and his daughter Susan serving as founding president and chief business officer. The latest funds will support the progress of Soufflé’s three lead programs into clinical studies, the most advanced of which is aimed at facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy. Release
October 7—Matchpoint Therapeutics
Series: B
Amount: $150M
Investors: Nextech Invest, Norwest, Invus, BB Biotech, T1D Fund, BOLD Longevity Growth, Access Biotechnology, Atlas Venture, Sanofi Ventures and Digitalis Ventures
The top spending priority for the series B funds will be taking MPT-062 into the clinic next year. MPT-062 is an oral T cell modulator that the biotech has been pitching as a first-in-class option to treat autoimmune and inflammatory diseases. Some money will also be set aside to advance Matchpoint’s “broader product pipeline”—although details remain under wraps for now. Story
October 6—KymaThera
Series: B
Amount: $80M
Investors: Alta Partners, Venrock, Foresite Capital, J. Wood Capital and others
The cash infusion will directly support the San Diego biotech's PI3Kα inhibitor K-1728, with a phase 1 trial planned to start dosing by the end of the year. The money should last through clinical proof-of-concept, and KymaThera plans to explore K-1728 as both a monotherapy and in combinations for HR+/HER2- breast cancer. Release
October 6—RougeTx
Series: A
Amount: $58M
Investors: BioGeneration Ventures, Angelini Ventures, Kurma Partners, Epidarex Capital, Vesalius Biocapital Partners, ROM Utrecht Region, p53 Invest and Kerna Ventures.
A new Dutch biotech has entered the world with plans to treat an inherited bleeding disorder called hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia. A spinout from Leiden University Medical Center, RougeTx will use the funds to push lead candidate RTX-001 into clinical development while also building up its small molecule vascular biology platform periSCOPE. Release
October 1—TriGemX Bio
Series: N/A
Amount: $94M
Investors: The Column Group, TCGX, Viking Global Investors and others.
Kallyope announced its new name, TriGemX Bio, along with a $94 million financing that will be used to advance the clinical development of the company’s lead program, elismetrep, an oral TRPM8 channel blocker currently in phase 3 trials for the acute treatment of migraine. The new name is a reference to the trigeminal ganglion, a migraine-relevant neural structure where TRPM8 is expressed. Elismetrep reduced migraine pain and associated symptoms in a phase 2b trial and was well tolerated. TriGemX is currently running a pivotal phase 3 study and a long-term safety study of elismetrep and plans to launch a second pivotal trial this month. Release

September 30—AnaCardio
Series: B
Amount: €62.5M ($70.9M)
Investors: Novo Holdings, the Ljungström family office, Helsinn, Pureos Bioventures, Sound Bioventures, Industrifonden, Flerie, LLD Nybohov Invest AB and Innovestor Life Science.
The Swedish biotech will use the funds to advance its oral calcium-sensitizing contractile agent, AC01, through phase 2b development in patients with chronic advanced heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, with the aim of establishing a phase 3-ready asset by 2028. AnaCardio was founded in 2017 out of the Karolinska Institutet and has raised around $114 million to date. Release
September 29—Breye
Series: A
Amount: $76.5M (€67.5M)
Investors: Mission BioCapital, Novo Holdings, Sound Bioventures, Ljungström family office, Innovestor Life Science, EIFO, Catalyze Capital, Modi Ventures and Pappas Capital
Novo-backed Breye Therapeutics closed an oversubscribed €67.5 million ($76.5 million) series A round to position the biotech advance its lead asset into phase 2 trials. The Copenhagen-based biotech is developing an oral small molecule to protect against retinal capillary breakdown and vascular leakage caused by chronically high glucose levels in non-proliferative diabetic retinopathy (NPDR). Breye was launched in November 2022 with a €4 million backing from Novo Holdings and Sound Bioventures. Release
September 23—Enveda
Series: E
Amount: $311M
Investors: Catalio Capital Management, Durable Capital Partners, ICONIQ, Lightspeed, Surveyor Capital (a Citadel company), Digitalis Ventures, a notable Sovereign Wealth Fund, and Alderline Group, Baillie Gifford, Premji Invest, FPV Ventures, True Ventures, Kinnevik, Dimension, Lifeforce Capital and Lux Capital
Enveda's latest $311 million raise will position the nature-derived drug discovery company to continue growing its model, which taps organisms for novel chemistry. The latest round follows annual raises that included a $130 million series C round in November 2024 and a $150 million series D round in September 2025. The cash flow has helped the company deliver clinical data on two drug candidates this year and begin testing a third molecule in humans. The company's assets are aimed at patients with eczema, atopic dermatitis and asthma. Story
September 23—Basecamp Research
Series: C
Amount: $140M
Investors: S32, Anthropic's Anthology Fund, Catalio Capital Management, European Tech Collective, Firebrand River Capital, Inception Fund, King Philanthropies, NATO Innovation Fund, NVIDIA, PostScriptum, Redalpine, The Rockefeller Foundation, Singular, Sovereign AI and True Ventures
Basecamp Research has raised an oversubscribed round to finance a new generation of its proprietary EDEN model and advance its pipeline of AI-designed therapeutics toward the clinic. EDEN is an in vivo cell therapy model designed to reprogram a patient’s cells inside the body. This model is trained on the company’s so-called Trillion Gene Atlas, a genomic dataset collected through partnerships in more than 30 countries. Basecamp’s pipeline includes preclinical cell, gene and peptide therapies focused on oncology, autoimmune disease, liver disease and diabetes. Release
September 16—Sling Therapeutics
Series: C
Amount: $123M
Investors: Forbion, TPG Life Sciences Innovations and Sectoral Asset Management.
Sling Therapeutics will use the funds to advance its oral therapy, linsitinib, for thyroid eye disease (TED) through a phase 3 trial and, if successful, into commercialization. Linsitinib was originally developed by Japanese pharma Astellas as a cancer treatment, but Sling licensed the therapy in 2022, while securing a $35 million series A to run a phase 2b trial of the drug in TED. Story
September 16—Circle Pharma
Series: E
Amount: $92.5M
Investors: The Column Group, Nextech Invest, RA Capital Management, Euclidean Capital and Eli Lilly and Company.
The Bay Area biotech founded in 2012 with seed funding from Pfizer, is focused on developing therapies targeted at cyclins, key regulators of the cell cycle that are implicated in many cancers. The funds from this financing round will drive the advancement of CID-165, Circle's oral macrocyclic cyclin D1 RxL inhibitor, through early clinical development for ER-positive breast cancer. Release
September 15—RegCell
Series: A and grant funding
Amount: $66M
Investors: Playground Global, Global Brain, Mitsui Chemicals, LG Technology Ventures, Alumni Ventures, Medical Incubator Japan, Fast Track Initiative, The University of Tokyo Edge Capital Partners, Osaka University Venture Capital, Kyoto iCAP and Japan’s Agency for Medical Research and Development.
Founded on the back of Nobel-winning regulatory T cell science, RegCell will use the raise to push its epigenetically reprogrammed Tregs into the clinic for autoimmune diseases. The company has secured a grant from Japan’s Agency for Medical Research and Development worth about $22 million, in addition to a $44 million series A. Release
September 9—Encoded Therapeutics
Series: F
Amount: $275M
Investors: Google Ventures, another healthcare fund, ARCH Venture Partners, Braidwell LP, Farallon Capital Management, Illumina Ventures, Invus, Janus Henderson Investors, Matrix Capital Management, Nolan Capital, RTW Investments, SoftBank Vision Fund 2 and Venrock.
The neuro-focused company will use the series F to support its Dravet syndrome asset and scale up its internal manufacturing capabilities. The proceeds will support a pivotal study of the asset, ETX101, in infants and young children with SCN1A-positive Dravet syndrome, a rare form of genetic epilepsy caused by mutations in the SCN1A gene. In addition to the commercial manufacturing build-out, the funding will also support a 2027 IND submission for ETX301, an AAV9-based vectorized microRNA gene therapy for post-amputation neuroma pain. Story
September 9—Solstice Oncology
Series: A
Amount: $225M
Investors: RA Capital Management, Canaan Partners, Forbion and others.
The Boston-based company licensed an Fc-enhanced cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) antibody, called porustobart, from China's Harbour BioMed back when it was founded in February. The series A will be used to fund the phase 2 trial testing porustobart with Keytruda. The study is due to start enrolling this year for a readout in the second half of 2027. Solstice also plans to develop porustobart in another, as-yet-undisclosed indication. Story
