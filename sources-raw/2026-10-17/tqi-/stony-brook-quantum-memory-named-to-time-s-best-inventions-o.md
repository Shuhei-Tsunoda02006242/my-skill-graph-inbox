Insider Brief
- Stony Brook University‘s patented quantum memory technology, commercialized by Qunnect as Qu-MEM, has been named to TIME’s Best Inventions of 2026 list in the Experimental category.
- Qu-MEM is a room-temperature quantum memory designed to store and retrieve quantum information and operate with existing fiber-optic infrastructure for quantum networking.
- Developed from research led by Stony Brook physicist Eden Figueroa and his former graduate students, Qu-MEM systems are being deployed on quantum networking testbeds in New York and Europe.
PRESS RELEASE — A quantum technology invented at the State University of New York (SUNY) at Stony Brook (Stony Brook University) is earning international recognition. Qunnect’s Qu-MEM, a commercial room-temperature quantum memory device developed from Stony Brook-patented technology, has been named to TIME’s Best Inventions of 2026 in the Experimental category for concept inventions and prototypes showing future potential. This competition recognizes inventions that make the world better, smarter and more impactful.
This recognition highlights Stony Brook’s role in advancing quantum technology and demonstrates how university research can move beyond the laboratory to help shape emerging industries.
Qunnect’s Qu-MEM grew out of research led by Professor Eden Figueroa, Stony Brook’s Endowed Presidential Professor of Physics and director of the Stony Brook University Quantum Institute alongside alumni/graduate students Mehdi Namazi, PhD ’18, and Mael Flament, MS ’19. Figueroa co-founded Qunnect with Namazi and Flament, who now serve as the company’s chief science officer and chief technology officer, respectively.
Qu-MEM is the world’s first commercial room-temperature quantum memory device, currently expanding Stony Brook’s SCY-QNET, America’s largest quantum internet. It is an on-demand, warm-vapor, neutral-atom quantum memory that sits in a standard server rack. It is designed to work with existing fiber-optic infrastructure, helping address a key challenge in building scalable quantum networks.
The patented technology is based on research at Stony Brook University and licensed to Qunnect, creating a direct link between the university’s scientific discoveries and commercial innovation with global potential.
SUNY Chancellor John B. King Jr. said, “The State University of New York at Stony Brook has emerged as a global leader in quantum technology and research through the strong investment by Governor Hochul, state leaders, and SUNY, and thanks to world-class researchers, faculty, and students who are moving this field forward. I applaud the State University of New York at Stony Brook on this well-earned recognition, which reinforces that investments made in SUNY research help move our state and society forward.”
“Stony Brook University is proud to celebrate Qunnect’s recognition by TIME for creating one of the year’s best inventions. Qu-MEM is a market-first quantum memory invention. Qunnect’s pioneering technology originated in the basement of our physics building by our visionary faculty member Eden Figueroa and his then-graduate students Mehdi Namazi and Mael Flament. The remarkable breakthrough of this quantum memory design enables it to store quantum data at room temperatures, which was thought to be impossible until Qunnect proved otherwise. This quantum memory is an essential component in Stony Brook’s fiber and wireless quantum network, which is the largest metropolitan area quantum network in the world communicating quantum information via entangled photons. This quantum network, which we plan to grow first to upstate New York and then throughout the country, is capable of protecting sensitive data in critical sectors including healthcare, finance and national defense,” said Stony Brook University President Andrea Goldsmith. “Qunnect’s technology is one shining example of how Stony Brook’s brilliant researchers are creating pathways for discoveries to move beyond the lab and into the marketplace, powering a vibrant ecosystem in academia, research and industry. From ideas sparked in our labs, to the testing and maturing of this technology, to building it into a real-world network with remarkable capabilities, this achievement is a testament to our university’s leadership and commitment to being one of the nation’s most impactful research institutions that further drives possibility and progress for the world.”
Quantum networks use properties such as photon entanglement to connect quantum devices and transmit information in ways that could enable new approaches to secure communications. By helping quantum signals work across fiber-optic networks, technologies such as Qu-MEM could contribute to the development of a future quantum internet.
“Early on, a lot of people doubted that a state university could pull off a breakthrough like this. But seeing our work featured in TIME alongside tech giants proves what’s possible. Quantum memory is what turns theoretical concepts like ‘spooky action at a distance’ into practical, long-distance technology. Overcoming our resource challenges to get here is a true testament to the exceptional talent and determination of our students. We built this from the ground up,” said Professor Figueroa, who also serves as an Associate Laboratory Director for the Innovation, Science, and Security Directorate at Brookhaven National Laboratory.
Today, Qu-MEM systems are being deployed on quantum networking testbeds across New York City, Long Island and Europe.
The impact of this technology is also already visible through its integration with the New York State Quantum Internet Testbed, which includes Brookhaven National Laboratory, public colleges throughout the SUNY system, and private universities including Yale and Columbia. Supported by more than $300 million in combined New York state and National Science Foundation investments, this growing network represents the largest quantum network in the United States and signals the potential for a scaled and virtually unhackable internet that reduces national data storage needs.
As a member of the Association of American Universities, Stony Brook is recognized for research and innovation across scientific disciplines. The development of Qu-MEM offers a powerful example of how discoveries made on campus – and the talent of the students who help drive them – can contribute to technological progress far beyond the University.
For more than 25 years, TIME Best Inventions has recognized innovations across science, technology and other fields that are shaping the future. Qu-MEM’s inclusion brings national attention to a technology rooted in Stony Brook research and to the university community working to move quantum networking from possibility to reality.
