Welcome to Fierce Biotech’s fifth annual layoff tracker, a resource designed to keep pace with the sector’s steady stream of workforce reductions.
In 2025, industry layoffs continued to rise year over year, prompting the need for another edition of this article.
As always, please reach out to the Fierce Biotech editorial team with any news of a biopharma workforce reduction. We are committed to protecting the anonymity of all tips, but we do conduct further reviews to verify all information we receive.
Editor’s note about the numbers: Layoff rounds that are part of a company’s larger, already-announced reorganization are not included in the monthly total count.
November—8 companies
November 13 - Milestone Pharmaceuticals: In a bid to reduce operating expenses by 30% while prioritizing sales force efforts for its heart condition nasal spray Cardamyst, Milestone said it was “reducing its headquarters workforce.” Release
November 13 - Cellectis: The French biotech's pivot from allogeneic CAR-Ts to in vivo therapies is set to come at the expense of 70% of its workforce. Cellectis entered 2026 with 229 full-time employees. Story
November 10 - Capricor Therapeutics: The San Diego-based biotech, which entered the year with 231 full-time employees, revealed it had laid off 10% of its workforce during the third quarter. The company said the layoffs were needed to “reduce operating expenses and align resources with its priorities.” Story
November 10 - NextCure: The biotech said it completed “significant restructuring initiatives” in the third quarter. A spokesperson told Fierce that these changes concerned a “substantial majority” of the company's workforce as part of aligning operations ahead of the merger with Avere Therapeutics. NextCure entered 2026 with 40 full-time employees. Story
November 10 - Vertex: Vertex has decided to lay off 78 employees at Crinetics Pharmaceuticals following its $10 billion acquisition of the endocrine disease drug developer. Story
November 9 - Coherus: Coherus Oncology is once again laying off 30% of its workforce as the biopharma narrows the focus of its investigational combo treatment to esophageal cancer. Last year, the company laid off a similar proportion of employees following the sale of its Neulasta biosimilar to Accord BioPharma. Story
November 5 - Agios: Cambridge, Massachusetts-based Agios Pharmaceuticals has been shrinking its R&D headcount over the course of 2026 as the commercial-stage company looks to eliminate more than 100 roles by year-end. Story
November 4 - Ultragenyx: The biopharma is laying off 20% of its workforce and axing drug development programs to cut its annual R&D budget to half of the amount it spent in 2025, while shifting focus to its commercial portfolio. Story
October—13 companies
October 30 - Molecular Partners: Zurich-based Molecular Partners will lay off up to 44% of its staff as it focuses its declining cash reserves on a radiotherapeutic pipeline led by an early-phase small cell lung cancer drug candidate. Story
October 29 - Avidity Biosciences: The San Diego-based RNA therapeutics biotech submitted a WARN notice affecting 40 employees effective by the end of 2026. Notice
October 29 - Lenz Therapeutics: Roughly one year into launch, Lenz Therapeutics, the maker of Vizz eye drops for presbyopia, will trim 75% of its workforce. The company's CEO is also headed for the exit. Story
October 28 - GSK: After revealing earlier in the month that up to 1,217 employees in Belgium are at risk of losing their jobs, executives confirmed that the pharma's wider restructuring would see “some limited impact” on roles in the British Big Pharma’s headquarters in London. Story
October 27 - Artiva Biotherapeutics: San Diego-based Artiva Biotherapeutics halted development of an autoimmune disease cell therapy and laid off 94% of its staff after two patients died and durability data fell short of expectations. Story
October 26 - Vistagen: The company reduced its headcount by 31% to preserve capital for its late clinical-stage priorities, which include phase 3 development of its social anxiety disorder candidate fasedienol. Filing
October 26 - Tango Therapeutics: Tango Therapeutics announced a 14% reduction in headcount even as the company celebrated more evidence that its PRMT5 inhibitor synergizes with Revolution Medicines’ Rasonque. Story
October 22 - Pfizer: The New York-headquartered pharma was reported to be cutting around 150 jobs from its biggest facility in Portage, Michigan. The company is also laying off an undisclosed number of employees at two North Carolina facilities: the Rocky Mount site, which manufactures sterile injectables, and Sanford, where Pfizer produces biologics, including gene therapies Story.
October 19 - Enlivex: Enlivex is laying off 60% of its workforce, ending preclinical R&D and trimming its top execs’ salaries as the Israeli biotech tries to keep cash flowing to its osteoarthritis drug. Story
October 19 - EyePoint: In an attempt to soothe investors and preserve capital after its phase 3 miss in wet age-related macular degeneration, EyePoint plans to reduce its workforce by 53% and cut both the Lucia trial and its companion phase 3 Lugano short. Story
October 6 - Caribou Biosciences: The off-the-shelf cell therapy outfit, which spun out of Nobel Laureate Jennifer Doudna’s lab in 2011, is halting development of its pipeline and laying off employees as it seeks strategic alternatives. Story
October 1 - Adecto Pharmaceuticals: In a LinkedIn post, the immunotherapy biotech blamed a “risk-averse funding environment” for shutting up shop. Story
October 1 - Foghorn Therapeutics: The Massachusetts biotech is laying off 40% of its workforce after a long-running collaboration with Eli Lilly collapsed over disappointing phase 1 data. Story
September—8 companies
September 30 - BioMarin Pharmaceuticals: BioMarin plans to cut 117 jobs at its Novato, California site in December, according to a WARN notice. The move follows BioMarin’s decision to consolidate its footprint in the California city into “a smaller, more focused site.” Story
September 29 - Enanta Pharmaceuticals: As antiviral specialist Enanta Pharmaceuticals endeavors to expand into immunology, the company has decided to lay off 37 employees. Story
September 23 - Bristol Myers Squibb: The Big Pharma has announced that it will eliminate 265 roles connected to its New Jersey headquarters, bringing the total number of job cuts this year to above 700 across three layoff rounds in the Garden State. Story
September 21 - Novo: The recently rebranded Danish drugmaker today revealed that following the already known departure of 9,000 employees last fall, a further 4,000 employees left the company as part of an ongoing drive to cut costs. Story
September 15 - Sionna Therapeutics: After the failure of its closely watched cystic fibrosis add-on program, the biotech is laying off almost half of its workforce and pivoting to development of another of its NBD1 stabilizers. Story
September 9 - Merck & Co.: In keeping with its 2027 cost-cutting deadline, the Big Pharma announced another round of 54 layoffs at its global headquarters in Rahway, New Jersey. Story
September 2 - Novartis: The Swiss pharma plans to discontinue small-volume biologics production at its Kleinbasel site by the end of 2027, a move that could eliminate approximately 130 jobs. Story
September 2 - TScan Therapeutics: TScan will reduce its workforce by 75% following the pause of its hematological and autoimmune programs. The company now plans to instead prioritize its preclinical in vivo solid tumor programs. Story
August—10 companies
August 31 - Arsenal Biosciences: About a year after halving its staff in a clinical push, the company is now making a hard pivot to in vivo CAR-T and laying off 99 employees in the process. Story
August 18 - Kolon TissueGene: Following a phase 3 knee osteoarthritis failure that its CEO acknowledged caused “great disappointment and concern,” the company is laying off 37 employees at its Maryland headquarters. Story
August 17 - Sanofi: In the first major workforce reduction disclosed under new CEO Belén Garijo, Sanofi is laying off 229 employees from its 2025 acquisition of Blueprint Medicines. Story
August 17 - Merck KGaA: The Massachusetts research site for Merck KGaA’s North American division, EMD Serono, is handing pink slips to 20 staffers. Story
August 11 - Aura Biosciences: The Boston biotech is refocusing under its recently installed CEO, laying off 20% of its employees and overhauling its C-suite to narrow its focus on ocular oncology. Story
August 10 - aTyr Pharma: Sixty per cent of the biotech's workforce are heading for the exits as the company conserves resources for a second shot at phase 3 success in a type of lung disease. aTyr will be left with around 20 full-time employees. Story
August 10 - Arpeggio Biosciences: In a LinkedIn post, the transcriptome biotech’s CEO admitted that the company’s Nrf2 program had begun to “unravel." The Colorado-based outfit's leadership decided to wind down operations last month. Story
August 7 - Ensoma: The Boston biotech is doubling down on its lead gene therapy for a rare immune disease, laying off another round of employees after previously halving its workforce last November. Story
August 5 - Emergent BioSolutions: The Narcan maker will lay off 93 employees across all areas of the company and remove 21 open positions in another round of restructuring to align the business with “current business realities.” Story
August 4 - Lisata: The New Jersey biotech laid off 72% of its staffers, including its chief medical officer, as it tries to navigate a new future after a planned merger with cancer imaging company Kuva Labs fell apart. Story
July—3 companies
July 24 - Amgen: The California pharma has laid off around 40 employees as part of “organizational changes” meant to better align operations with the company’s strategic objectives. Story
July 23 - Clinuvel Pharmaceuticals: The Australian biopharma is moving its headquarters to the U.S., but up to a fifth of the company’s workforce won’t be staying for the trans-pacific pivot. Story
July 7 - Novartis: The latest round of layoffs at the Swiss pharma's U.S. headquarters in East Hanover, New Jersey sees 322 pink slips sent out, mainly affecting the field sales, patient support and marketing organizations. Story
July 6 - Genentech: A month after confirming the restructuring of its early development group (see June entry below), the Roche subsidiary has now disclosed that this change has formed part of a round of 103 layoffs. Story
June—8 companies
June 29 - BioCryst: A month after ending the development of its diabetic macular edema treatment avoralstat, the company announced it was shuttering all internal discovery programs to focus on external innovation and partnerships. The change will result in the closure of BioCryst's Discovery Center of Excellence facility in Birmingham, Alabama by the end of this year, though the biopharma didn't share how many positions would be impacted. Story
June 26 - Biogen: About a month after finalizing its Apellis buyout, Biogen is shrinking the R&D vision of the acquired biotech. The company is ending most of Apellis’ research programs and eliminating a “small number” of research roles, a spokesperson told Fierce. The changes will allow Biogen to focus on Apellis’ approved drugs Syfovre and Empaveli. Story
June 24 - ADC Therapeutics: In the wake of a recent safety scare in a confirmatory trial of its antibody-drug conjugate Zynlonta, the Swiss ADC specialist unveiled a reorganization scheme that includes layoffs for around 17% of the company's global workforce. Story
June 23 - Sangamo Therapeutics: The beleaguered genomic medicine biotech is selling off assets to Eli Lilly and Astellas Pharma while also saying goodbye to around 51 employees as part of a bankruptcy process. Story
