Australian quantum software company QFactorial and Monash University have secured approximately A$1.12 million ($758.6K USD) under Round 2 Stage 2 of the Australian Department of Industry, Science and Resources’ Critical Technologies Challenge Program (CTCP) to advance a hybrid quantum-classical optimization solver for freight and last-mile delivery. Selected as one of six Stage 2 demonstrator projects out of 14 initial feasibility awardees, the 15-month initiative aims to progress QFactorial’s routing and fleet-utilization platform from Technology Readiness Level (TRL) 5 to TRL 7.
| Australian CTCP Round 2 Stage 2 Quantum Logistics Consortium |  |  | 
|---|---|---|
| Consortium Role | Participating Organizations | Technical & Operational Scope | 
| Lead Software Developer | QFactorial Pty Ltd | Scales proprietary quantum optimization heuristics for dynamic route planning and vehicle load management. | 
| Academic R&D Partners | Monash University, University of Queensland, Griffith University | Develops circuit-tailored quantum error correction, noise mitigation, and AI-driven problem modeling. | 
| Compute Infrastructure | IBM Quantum, Amazon Web Services (AWS), Fujitsu | Provides multi-platform quantum and classical execution backends for 15-month hardware trials. | 
| Commercial End-Users | Team Global Express, Opal Packaging | Supplies operational freight and packaging datasets to validate commercial deployment workflows. | 
Combinatorial vehicle routing, load balancing, and fleet scheduling problems often exceed the time limits of exact classical solvers when operational constraints scale across national supply chains. Within the consortium, Monash University researchers led by Professor Muhammad Usman will focus on improving algorithmic efficiency and designing circuit-specific error correction to suppress hardware noise on near-term quantum processors.
According to QFactorial Chief Scientist and Co-Founder Tavis Bennett, integrating tailored error-mitigation routines directly into the company’s optimization circuits is essential for scaling hybrid solvers to production workloads. Beyond logistics, CTCP Round 2 Stage 2 also awarded demonstrator grants to Silicon Quantum Computing, QuantX Labs, Avicena Systems, iQ Sense, and FeBI Technologies across energy grid synchronization and quantum biosensing challenges.
