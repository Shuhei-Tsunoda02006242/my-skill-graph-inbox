Welcome to Fierce Biotech’s fifth annual layoff tracker, a resource designed to keep pace with the sector’s steady stream of workforce reductions.
In 2025, industry layoffs continued to rise year over year, prompting the need for another edition of this article.
As always, please reach out to the Fierce Biotech editorial team with any news of a biopharma workforce reduction. We are committed to protecting the anonymity of all tips, but we do conduct further reviews to verify all information we receive.
      
      
Editor’s note about the numbers: Layoff rounds that are part of a company’s larger, already-announced reorganization are not included in the monthly total count.
 
September—4 companies
September 15 - Sionna Therapeutics: After the failure of its closely watched cystic fibrosis add-on program, the biotech is laying off almost half of its workforce and pivoting to development of another of its NBD1 stabilizers. Story
September 9 - Merck & Co.: In keeping with its 2027 cost-cutting deadline, the Big Pharma announced another round of 54 layoffs at its global headquarters in Rahway, New Jersey. Story
      
September 2 - Novartis: The Swiss pharma plans to discontinue small-volume biologics production at its Kleinbasel site by the end of 2027, a move that could eliminate approximately 130 jobs. Story
September 2 - TScan Therapeutics: TScan will reduce its workforce by 75% following the pause of its hematological and autoimmune programs. The company now plans to instead prioritize its preclinical in vivo solid tumor programs. Story
August—10 companies
August 31 - Arsenal Biosciences: About a year after halving its staff in a clinical push, the company is now making a hard pivot to in vivo CAR-T and laying off 99 employees in the process. Story
August 18 - Kolon TissueGene: Following a phase 3 knee osteoarthritis failure that its CEO acknowledged caused “great disappointment and concern,” the company is laying off 37 employees at its Maryland headquarters. Story
August 17 - Sanofi: In the first major workforce reduction disclosed under new CEO Belén Garijo, Sanofi is laying off 229 employees from its 2025 acquisition of Blueprint Medicines. Story
August 17 - Merck KGaA: The Massachusetts research site for Merck KGaA’s North American division, EMD Serono, is handing pink slips to 20 staffers. Story
August 11 - Aura Biosciences: The Boston biotech is refocusing under its recently installed CEO, laying off 20% of its employees and overhauling its C-suite to narrow its focus on ocular oncology. Story
August 10 - aTyr Pharma: Sixty per cent of the biotech's workforce are heading for the exits as the company conserves resources for a second shot at phase 3 success in a type of lung disease. aTyr will be left with around 20 full-time employees. Story
August 10 - Arpeggio Biosciences: In a LinkedIn post, the transcriptome biotech’s CEO admitted that the company’s Nrf2 program had begun to “unravel." The Colorado-based outfit's leadership decided to wind down operations last month. Story
August 7 - Ensoma: The Boston biotech is doubling down on its lead gene therapy for a rare immune disease, laying off another round of employees after previously halving its workforce last November. Story
      
August 5 - Emergent BioSolutions: The Narcan maker will lay off 93 employees across all areas of the company and remove 21 open positions in another round of restructuring to align the business with “current business realities.” Story
August 4 - Lisata: The New Jersey biotech laid off 72% of its staffers, including its chief medical officer, as it tries to navigate a new future after a planned merger with cancer imaging company Kuva Labs fell apart. Story
July—3 companies
July 24 - Amgen: The California pharma has laid off around 40 employees as part of “organizational changes” meant to better align operations with the company’s strategic objectives. Story
July 23 - Clinuvel Pharmaceuticals: The Australian biopharma is moving its headquarters to the U.S., but up to a fifth of the company’s workforce won’t be staying for the trans-pacific pivot. Story
July 7 - Novartis: The latest round of layoffs at the Swiss pharma's U.S. headquarters in East Hanover, New Jersey sees 322 pink slips sent out, mainly affecting the field sales, patient support and marketing organizations. Story
July 6 - Genentech: A month after confirming the restructuring of its early development group (see June entry below), the Roche subsidiary has now disclosed that this change has formed part of a round of 103 layoffs. Story
      
June—8 companies
June 29 - BioCryst: A month after ending the development of its diabetic macular edema treatment avoralstat, the company announced it was shuttering all internal discovery programs to focus on external innovation and partnerships. The change will result in the closure of BioCryst's Discovery Center of Excellence facility in Birmingham, Alabama by the end of this year, though the biopharma didn't share how many positions would be impacted. Story
June 26 - Biogen: About a month after finalizing its Apellis buyout, Biogen is shrinking the R&D vision of the acquired biotech. The company is ending most of Apellis’ research programs and eliminating a “small number” of research roles, a spokesperson told Fierce. The changes will allow Biogen to focus on Apellis’ approved drugs Syfovre and Empaveli. Story
June 24 - ADC Therapeutics: In the wake of a recent safety scare in a confirmatory trial of its antibody-drug conjugate Zynlonta, the Swiss ADC specialist unveiled a reorganization scheme that includes layoffs for around 17% of the company's global workforce. Story
June 23 - Sangamo Therapeutics: The beleaguered genomic medicine biotech is selling off assets to Eli Lilly and Astellas Pharma while also saying goodbye to around 51 employees as part of a bankruptcy process. Story
June 15 - enGene: As enGene works toward a potential FDA filing for its bladder cancer gene therapy detalimogene, the company is trimming ranks to preserve cash. In a Monday release, the company detailed plans to cut half its workforce. It's also parting ways with several high-profile execs. The cuts are intended to allow the company to stay nimble until it can get its hands on 12-month data for the candidate and meet with the FDA regarding a Biologics License Application. EnGene anticipates submitting an approval filing for the gene therapy prospect this year. Release
June 15 - Neumora Therapeutics: Alongside reporting Monday that navacaprant failed a pair of phase 3 trials, Neumora said it's cutting around 35% of its staff. The reductions are aimed at saving around $10 million annually, with one-time reorganization expenses projected to hit $2 million. Neumora is now focusing on assets in Alzheimer's disease agitation, schizophrenia and obesity. Story
      
June 11 - Genentech: Vice presidents Vishva Dixit, M.D., Man-Wah Tan, Ph.D., and Todd McDevitt, Ph.D., were among the casualties in another round of layoffs at the South San Francisco subsidiary of Roche. The departures are part of a restructuring of the Genentech Research and Early Development (gRED) group. A spokesperson would not confirm how many positions are affected, but said “targeted adjustments” are being made to “ensure our capabilities and investments are aligned with our core therapeutic areas and portfolio priorities.” Story
June 4 - Fulcrum Therapeutics: After scrapping its only clinical-stage program following a hardline stance from the FDA, the sickle cell disease-focused biotech is drastically reducing its workforce by 85% as it begins searching for strategic alternatives. Filing and Story
May—4 companies
May 15 - Novartis: After a series of staff consolidation moves in recent weeks, the Swiss pharma is again trimming its ranks, albeit in what appears to be a more limited move centered on the drugmaker’s biomedical research arm. Story
May 13 - Valneva: The French vaccine maker's “further restructuring plan [is] designed to streamline its global business operations.” It comes against the backdrop of “an emerging adverse trend in travel vaccine uptake across key markets,” and will include a reduction in global headcount of between 10% and 15%. Story
May 13 - Takeda: The Japanese pharma filled in details on its 2026 “transformation program,” which looks to centralize corporate functions, reduce management layers and target process simplification. The multiyear slim-down is expected to hit about 4,500 roles during the 2026 fiscal year. Story
May 13 - Gilead: With the ink now dry on the $7.8 billion buyout of CAR-T biotech Arcellx, Gilead will lay off 108 workers from Arcellx’s site at 800 Bridge Parkway in Redwood City, CA, and 84 employees at the 800 King Farm Boulevard site in Rockville, MD. Story
May 5 - BioNTech: In the face of shrinking COVID-19 vaccine sales, German company BioNTech is slashing its manufacturing footprint, cutting 1,860 jobs while pulling out of its manufacturing sites in Singapore, the German cities of Idar-Oberstein and Marburg as well as at its CureVac subsidiary’s former headquarters in Tübingen. Story
      
April—4 companies
April 30 - Autolus Therapeutics: The CAR-T cell therapy outfit is shrinking its staff by 13% across the business as part of a plan to cut costs. The cuts will “enhance our margins, support scalable growth, and position Autolus for long-term value creation,” CEO Christian Itin, Ph.D., said. Release
April 28 - Passage Bio: After some tough feedback from the FDA on the path forward for its lead gene therapy, Passage Bio has launched a strategic review and set out a plan to whittle down its headcount by 75%. Story
April 21 - Replimune: The oncology biotech is shedding an additional 161 employees, or 60% of its workers, across two Massachusetts sites as the fallout continues from another FDA rejection of its lead melanoma candidate. Story
April 14 - Astellas Pharma: The Japanese pharma is preparing to close the Seattle site of its stem cell therapy unit, with around 55 employees set to be impacted. Story
April 13 - Replimune: After facing a "disappointing" second rejection for its melanoma prospect, the Massachusetts biotech is saying goodbye to 63 employees. Story
March—16 companies
March 31 - Iterum Therapeutics: The antibiotic specialist has started winding down its operations, citing limited cash amid the expensive commercialization of its only approved product, Orlynvah. Story
      
March 31 - IO Biotech: After its cancer vax was spurned by the FDA and following deep layoffs, IO Biotech is closing up shop for good, terminating all employees and executives. Story
March 31 - Lipella Pharmaceuticals: The Pittsburgh-based company's ambition to develop the first treatment for an inflammatory mouth condition has reached the end of the road, with the biotech filing for bankruptcy. Story
March 30 - Takeda: Takeda plans to cut 634 jobs in the U.S. as part of a massive restructuring designed to save the company more than 200 billion Japanese yen ($1.26 billion) annually. The roles are assigned to the company’s U.S. headquarters in Cambridge, Massachusetts, and include 247 that are currently located in the Bay State, according to a WARN notice. Employee notifications have begun, but the changes will not take effect until July 2026, after new CEO Julie Kim officially takes the helm. Story
March 17 - Gossamer Bio: The biotech is cutting its workforce by 48% after the company’s phase 3 study of seralutinib in patients with pulmonary arterial hypertension missed its primary endpoint. Gossamer is evaluating strategic options and conserving cash with the 77-role reduction. Source
March 17 - Bicycle Therapeutics: The biotech is letting more air out of its operations, with plans to deflate its employee count by about 30% and shelve its lead anti-cancer drug conjugate. The reprioritization is meant to halve yearly operating costs and will likely impact about 86 roles. Story
