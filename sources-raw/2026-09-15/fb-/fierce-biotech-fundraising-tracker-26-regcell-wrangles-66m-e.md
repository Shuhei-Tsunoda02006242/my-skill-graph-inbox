A new year brings a fresh Fierce Biotech Fundraising Tracker designed to record the significant amount of venture capital that flows into biopharma.
We’re keeping the same guidelines used for last year’s tracker and will only include financing rounds of $50 million and above.
September
September 15—RegCell
Series: A and grant funding
Amount: $66M
Investors: Playground Global, Global Brain, Mitsui Chemicals, LG Technology Ventures, Alumni Ventures, Medical Incubator Japan, Fast Track Initiative, The University of Tokyo Edge Capital Partners, Osaka University Venture Capital, Kyoto iCAP and Japan’s Agency for Medical Research and Development.
      
      
Founded on the back of Nobel-winning regulatory T cell science, RegCell will use the raise to push its epigenetically reprogrammed Tregs into the clinic for autoimmune diseases. The company has secured a grant from Japan’s Agency for Medical Research and Development worth about $22 million, in addition to a $44 million series A. Release
September 9—Encoded Therapeutics
Series: F
Amount: $275M
Investors: Google Ventures, another healthcare fund, ARCH Venture Partners, Braidwell LP, Farallon Capital Management, Illumina Ventures, Invus, Janus Henderson Investors, Matrix Capital Management, Nolan Capital, RTW Investments, SoftBank Vision Fund 2 and Venrock.
The neuro-focused company will use the series F to support its Dravet syndrome asset and scale up its internal manufacturing capabilities. The proceeds will support a pivotal study of the asset, ETX101, in infants and young children with SCN1A-positive Dravet syndrome, a rare form of genetic epilepsy caused by mutations in the SCN1A gene. In addition to the commercial manufacturing build-out, the funding will also support a 2027 IND submission for ETX301, an AAV9-based vectorized microRNA gene therapy for post-amputation neuroma pain. Story
      
September 9—Solstice Oncology
Series: A
Amount: $225M
Investors: RA Capital Management, Canaan Partners, Forbion and others.
The Boston-based company licensed an Fc-enhanced cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) antibody, called porustobart, from China's Harbour BioMed back when it was founded in February. The series A will be used to fund the phase 2 trial testing porustobart with Keytruda. The study is due to start enrolling this year for a readout in the second half of 2027. Solstice also plans to develop porustobart in another, as-yet-undisclosed indication. Story
September 9—TwoStep Therapeutics
Series: A
Amount: $62.5M
Investors: Insight Partners, Medical Excellence Capital, M Ventures (the venture capital arm of Merck KGaA), Pfizer Ventures, NFX, 2048 Ventures, Stanford University and others.
With a new pair of Big Pharma backers, TwoStep is looking to dance its lead peptide-drug conjugate into the clinic later this year for solid tumors. TS-104 combines TwoStep's multi-targeting technique, a polyspecific integrin-binding peptide or PIP, with the potent chemo payload monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE). The company counts Carolyn Bertozzi, Ph.D., the Stanford chemist and 2022 Nobel Laureate, among its co-founders. Release
September 9—Tectora Therapeutics
Series: A
Amount: $55M
Investors: New Enterprise Associates and RA Capital Management.
The new immune disease-focused Tectora has spun out from Schrödinger with two early-stage small molecule programs. Tectora will partner with Schrödinger’s drug discovery experts and leverage Schrödinger’s computational platform to help advance these candidates to the clinic. The programs, SDGR-4594 and SDGR-8139, are currently in undisclosed indications but are products of Schrödinger’s computational platform. Story
September 9—Caspian Therapeutics
Series: N/A
Amount: $50M
Investors: BVF Partners, T1D Fund, Invus, Montanova, Eli Lilly and Company, Kura Oncology, members of Kura’s leadership team and several other biotech entrepreneurs.
The new Kura Oncology spinout will be focused on KO-7246, a highly selective menin inhibitor that Kura has billed as being “purpose-designed for chronic metabolic diseases.” A portion of the funding has also been earmarked to support development of another menin inhibitor aimed at diabetes and other cardiometabolic diseases. Story
September 8—BrainChild Bio
Series: A
Amount: $116M
Investors: Undisclosed private family fund and foundation, Seattle Children’s and WRF Capital
Three years after pediatric oncologist Michael Jensen, M.D., launched BrainChild Bio out of Seattle Children’s Hospital, the company has raised a series A round to support the completion of a pivotal phase 2 trial for its lead candidate, BCB-276, in a childhood brainstem cancer called diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG). Radiation is the current standard of care and is mostly palliative, so the company hopes to develop the first DIPG-specific treatment by taking T cells from patients, genetically modifying them to attack cancer then reinfusing them into the central nervous system. The biotech has a second program focused on glioblastoma, an aggressive brain cancer with a much larger patient pool that also includes adults, and plans to launch a phase 1 trial in that indication next year. Story
      
September 8—Moonwalk Biosciences
Series: B
Amount: $70M
Investors: Alpha Wave, YK Bioventures, Eli Lilly, Gaorong Ventures, Arch Venture Partners, Khosla Ventures and Future Ventures
Moonwalk Biosciences will deploy the funds to advance adipose-targeted RNA interference (RNAi) medicines for obesity and cardiometabolic diseases. After launching in 2024 with a $57 million series A round, the South San Francisco- and San Diego-based genomic biotech plans to use the latest funding to move the company’s lead obesity candidate, MW101, into the clinic late next year. The proceeds are also intended to support Moonwalk’s pipeline of tissue-targeted siRNA medicines and advance its discovery platform, which aims to selectively deliver siRNA therapeutics directly to adipose tissue, enabling precise modulation of novel, non-incretin pathways underlying obesity. Release
September 3—NeuShen Therapeutics
Series: B
Amount: $80M
Investors: B Capital, Legend Capital, Matrix Partners China, TruMed Investment, SSCI and Lilly Asia Ventures
The Shanghai and Boston-based biotech will use the funds to advance its clinical-stage, central nervous system-focused candidates, including a non-hallucinogenic 5-HT2A receptor partial agonist for treatment-resistant depression, as well as an M4 receptor positive allosteric modulator being evaluated for both schizophrenia and agitation associated with Alzheimer's disease. There's also a KCNQ2/3 channel opener for both focal onset seizures and major depressive disorder. Release
September 3—Superluminal Medicines
Series: B
Amount: $60M
Investors: BVF Partners L.P., Deep Track Capital, Perceptive Advisors, RA Capital Management, Insight Partners, NVIDIA, Catalio Capital Management, Eli Lilly and Company, Cooley and Gaingels.
The cash will supercharge Superluminal's phase 1 plans for an MC4R agonist in development for genetic forms of obesity and hypothalamic obesity. The trial is expected to kick off later this year. The raise is also meant to help the Boston biotech build up its pipeline of specially crafted small molecules that target G protein-coupled receptors. Release

August 28—AusperBio
Series: C
Amount: $120M
Investors: RA Capital Management, HanKang Capital, Sherpa Capital, InnoPinnacle Fund, Qiming Venture Partners, YuanBio Venture Capital and CDH Investments.
      
The funds will be used to advance the company's oligonucleotide therapy for chronic hepatitis B through phase 3 testing and hopefully on to commercialization. The Bay Area biotech's lead candidate, dubbed AHB-137, is an unconjugated antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) designed to suppress production of viral hepatitis B protein, inhibit viral DNA replication and promote immune reactivation. The therapy is currently undergoing a late-stage study in China. Chronic hepatitis B, caused by the hepatitis B virus, affects nearly 296 million people worldwide. Story
August 18—Abcuro
Series: D
Amount: $66M
Investors: New Leaf Venture Partners, Bain Capital Life Sciences, Samsara BioCapital, Redmile Group, Mass General Brigham Ventures, RA Capital Management, Pontifax, Sanofi Ventures, Foresite Capital, NEA, Eurofarma Ventures, Kaitai Capital, Soleus Capital, Nancy Chang, Shang Bay, Rock Springs Capital.
Autoimmune disease-focused Abcuro will use the funds to support a new clinical study of ulviprubart, its leading monoclonal antibody targeting T cells, for the treatment of patients with less severe inclusion body myositis (IBM). In late March, Abcuro reported ulviprubart data in a phase 2/3 trial in a broader population of patients with IBM. Although the study did not reach statistical significance for slowing of the disease, the company noted a positive signal in a pre-specified subgroup of patients with less severe disease. Release
August 12—InduPro
Series: B
Amount: $77M
Investors: The Column Group, Vida Ventures, MRL Ventures Fund (the therapeutics-focused venture fund of Merck & Co., Inc.), Emerson Collective (advised by Yosemite), Euclidean Capital, Solasta Ventures, Sanofi and Eli Lilly and Company.
InduPro has assembled a syndicate of Big Pharma backers to push its lead cancer candidate IDP-001 through a phase 1 trial that just dosed its first patient. IDP-001 is a bispecific antibody-drug conjugate targeting EGFR and a novel tumor-associated proximity antigen (TAPA). The first-in-human trial is testing the molecule in advanced or metastatic squamous or non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer and other squamous solid tumors with disease progression. Release
August 11—Vaderis Therapeutics
Series: B
Amount: $152M
Investors: Life Sciences at Goldman Sachs Alternatives, TCGX, Omega Funds, EQT Life Sciences, Perceptive Advisors, Kalehua Capital, Medicxi and Droia.
The new cash will propel the Swiss biotech's AKT inhibitor through phase 3 with the aim of bringing the first treatment for a rare blood vessel disorder to market. The Basel-based company has just launched a late-stage study to evaluate the once-daily oral drug, called engasertib, in patients with hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia. There are currently no approved therapies for the rare genetic vascular disorder, which affects one in every 3,800 people. Story
August 11—Epicrispr Biotechnologies
Series: C
Amount: $90M
Investors: Octagon Capital, Janus Henderson Investors, Fidelity Management & Research Company, Cormorant Asset Management, Duquesne Family Office, Sanofi Ventures, funds managed by abrdn Inc., Angelini Ventures, Readout Capital and existing investors.
The funds position the biotech to advance a pipeline of epigenetic medicines led by an early-phase facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy (FSHD) prospect. San Francisco-based Epicrispr began a phase 1/2 trial of the FSHD drug candidate, EPI-321, last year. A recent look at data from the trial showed increases in lean muscle mass in three patients with FSHD, a rare muscle degeneration disease. Story
      
August 11—Infinimmune
Series: A
Amount: $75M
Investors: Regeneron Ventures, Playground Global, RA Capital Management, Goldcrest Capital, Forge Life Science Partners, Godfrey Capital, Wild Tree Ventures, Merck Global Health Innovation Fund, Everbright Biofund, Civilization Ventures and Pear VC.
The antibody upstart will use the raise to push two eczema candidates into the clinic next year. IFX-101 is designed to hit interleukin-22, which has never been drugged before, while IFX-201 targets interleukin-13, which is also a target of Regeneron and Sanofi's Dupixent. Story
