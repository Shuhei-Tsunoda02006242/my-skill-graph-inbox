Heidi Overton, President Trump’s nominee to lead the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, was grilled by senators on hot button topics like China, vaccines, and abortion access in her hearing before the committee that will decide whether to send her nomination for a vote of the full Senate, but revealed very little of her own feelings on top issues facing the agency, STAT writes. Instead, she referred repeatedly to existing agency guidance and approvals for products like childhood vaccines and the abortion drug mifepristone — without saying whether she would endorse updating those guidances or altering approvals to satisfy the desires of Trump or health secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.
Novo Nordisk agreed to pay up to $1.3 billion to license and develop Nanexa’s drug delivery technology in a deal that could cut the frequency that patients need to inject its obesity and diabetes drugs, The Wall Street Journal writes. The deal covers Nanexa’s PharmaShell technology, which is designed to enable controlled and sustained release of active pharmaceutical ingredients through an ultra-thin coating applied to individual drug particles. Under the exclusive deal, Novo licensed Nanexa’s technology in up to five development programs covering different drug administration frequencies, with the companies targeting monthly and quarterly administration.
