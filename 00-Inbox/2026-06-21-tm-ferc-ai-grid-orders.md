## Key Claim
On June 18, FERC issued tailored show-cause orders to all six major US grid operators — covering 200 million Americans across 30+ states — directing them to reform interconnection tariffs for AI data centers and other large-load customers, treating AI power demand as a national infrastructure priority requiring federal coordination.

## Evidence / Context
- Unanimous 5-0 FERC vote; orders issued under Section 206 of the Federal Power Act
- Six grid operators targeted: PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO-NE, NYISO
- Each operator must justify or reform tariff provisions governing how data centers connect to and receive transmission service
- New requirements include: better cost information for states, faster interconnection services, expanded accommodation for co-location arrangements (on-site power next to data centers)
- Ratepayer costs shielded — large customers must bear their own interconnection costs
- Action triggered by Secretary of Energy Chris Wright's October 2025 directive ordering FERC to address AI/crypto mining power demand on grid stability
- FERC framing: AI power demand is "no longer a private-sector scheduling problem — it is a national infrastructure crisis requiring federal coordination"
- Region-by-region approach rather than single national rule, due to differing market designs
