## Key Claim
US Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick told senior ASML executives he believes one of their banned EUV lithography machines may have reached China in violation of longstanding export controls — a claim ASML flatly denies, saying all 314 of its EUV systems are fully accounted for.

## Evidence / Context
- Each EUV machine costs $150M–$400M and contains over 100,000 precision components
- ASML is the sole manufacturer of EUV tools on the planet; they are required for printing the world's most advanced chip nodes
- EUV has been banned from export to China since the first Trump administration
- US officials raised the concern in bilateral meetings but reportedly declined to share supporting evidence
- Without EUV access, Chinese chipmakers (e.g. SMIC) are capped below ~7nm process nodes — a direct constraint on their most advanced AI and military chips
- The allegation follows mounting US scrutiny of semiconductor equipment leakage to China
