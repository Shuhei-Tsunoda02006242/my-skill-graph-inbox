## Key Claim
Imperagen, a University of Manchester spin-out, uses quantum physics-based simulation to computationally predict enzyme variant behavior across millions of mutations — bypassing wet-lab trial-and-error — and feeds the results into a custom AI in a closed loop that has demonstrated over 500× enzyme productivity improvements.

## Evidence / Context
- Raised £5M ($6.7M) seed round led by PXN Ventures; participants: IQ Capital, Northern Gritstone
- Total funding to date: £8.5M ($11.4M)
- Founded 2021 by Dr. Andrew Currin, Dr. Tim Eyes, Dr. Andy Almond — Manchester Institute of Biotechnology scientists
- Technology stack: quantum physics modeling (millions of mutations simulated in silico) → custom AI models trained on enzyme-specific data → robotic/automated lab validation → data fed back to AI (closed-loop)
- Key result: >500× enzyme productivity gain after repeated optimization cycles in commercial projects
- New CEO: Guy Levy-Yurista (announced alongside funding)
- Target sectors: pharmaceuticals, life sciences, personal care, industrial biotechnology
- Funds will hire AI specialists, expand R&D platform, and build go-to-market over 18 months
