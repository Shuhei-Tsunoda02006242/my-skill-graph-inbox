## Key Claim
AI data center demand for High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) has diverted 23% of global DRAM wafer capacity away from standard DRAM, triggering a memory shortage dubbed "RAM-ageddon" that is pushing up consumer electronics prices and threatening the survival of thin-margin device makers.

## Evidence / Context
- HBM now consumes ~23% of total DRAM wafer starts, a figure that could rise further as AI training and inference scale
- Q1 2026: average memory prices up 40%+ year-over-year; some product categories saw 1,000% price inflation
- Mid-range devices spend 15–20% of total bill-of-materials on memory — price spikes flow directly into device COGS
- Memory suppliers imposing prepayment requirements and shorter payment windows, squeezing working capital for smaller OEMs
- Companies impacted: Apple (Mac price hikes), TCL, Transsion, Realme, Xiaomi, Lenovo, Oppo, Vivo, Honor, Huawei — all thin-margin consumer electronics brands
- IDC flagged "existential threats" to low-end smartphone makers unable to pass costs to price-sensitive consumers
- Samsung and SK Hynix benefit from premium HBM pricing while cutting commodity DRAM supply
