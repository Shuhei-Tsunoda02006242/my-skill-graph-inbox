## Key Claim
Microsoft's Majorana 2 processor achieves qubit parity lifetimes of ~22 seconds (occasionally reaching minutes), a 1,000x improvement over prior 1–12 millisecond durations, by replacing aluminum with lead in its superconducting material stack — announced at Build 2026 on June 2.

## Evidence / Context
- Previous Majorana-based devices: parity lifetime 1–12 milliseconds; Majorana 2: characteristic parity lifetime ~22 seconds, occasional minute-long durations
- Material change: aluminum → lead in the superconducting stack; redesigned semiconductor structure more than doubled the topological gap protecting qubits from errors
- The improvement was accelerated using Microsoft Discovery, an agentic AI system that identified the lead-based formulation
- Microsoft's stated roadmap: scalable, fault-tolerant quantum computer by 2029, integrated with Azure Quantum
- Context: announcement followed external debate over Microsoft's 2025 topological qubit claims; Science News noted critics remain skeptical about commercial applicability at scale
- Parallel milestones: QuiX Quantum deployed Feed-Forward Control Unit enabling 150 ns latency adaptive photonic quantum operations; Japan also advancing fault-tolerance research
